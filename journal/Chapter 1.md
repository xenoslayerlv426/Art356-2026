# Chapter 1

One of the topics I found interesting in this chapter was the “Paradox of Technology”. An example Norman uses to demonstrate this is your average watch and how simple it was to use based off of just twisting the knob to change the time. Over time with the development of new technology, smartphones came along with the same feature somewhat making watches obsolete due to it also displaying the time. We now have smartwatches with many features, but like Norman says on page 34, “The added functions cause problems”. When so many features are introduced, it becomes complicated to operate, which is just against a watches original purpose, to just tell the time. Thus enacting a paradox of technology. Another topic I found interesting was “The System Image”. This goes over the importance of good design and how well you can convey a model without talking to them. When they see the model, they should be able to understand how it works and use it without any issues. An example I have of this was when my family and I had gotten a new microwave. It is the one where you push a button in order to open instead of a handle. The issue, when pushing the button, nothing happened. It took a couple of minutes to figure out that it had a childlock feature already set on it. The microwave was simple, but I feel that the feature was barely visible on the appliance, causing an unnecessary issue that made us think it was defective.
